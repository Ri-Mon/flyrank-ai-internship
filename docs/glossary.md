## Centaur Tasks

**Plain explanation:** A term from Ethan Mollick's task-delegation framework — work where AI stays woven into the process through iteration, not handed a finished task to check at the end. The name comes from "centaur chess," where human + AI working together in real time beats either alone. Maps directly to FlyRank's "Collaborate with AI" category.

**Where I've used/seen it:** [FL-01 Workflow Audit](../ai-fluency/week-01-proof-statement/fl-01-workflow-audit.md) — used to classify 6 of my 12 recurring tasks (debugging, brainstorming, writing code, clarifying requirements).

**Source(s):** Ethan Mollick, ["On-boarding your AI Intern"](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/on-boarding-your-ai-intern)

## Delegated Tasks

**Plain explanation:** Work handed off to AI with a defined scope, then checked at the end — not iterated on together in real time. The key difference from a Centaur Task is *timing*: AI works, then I review, rather than us working through it side by side.

**Where I've used/seen it:** [FL-01 Workflow Audit](../ai-fluency/week-01-proof-statement/fl-01-workflow-audit.md) — 2 of my 12 tasks (researching new concepts, organizing/documenting work).

**Source(s):** Ethan Mollick, ["On-boarding your AI Intern"](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/on-boarding-your-ai-intern)

## Automated Tasks

**Plain explanation:** Work handed to AI with no human review at all. Deliberately a small, risky category, not something to default into just because it saves time — the stakes have to be genuinely low enough that an unchecked mistake doesn't matter.

**Where I've used/seen it:** [FL-01 Workflow Audit](../ai-fluency/week-01-proof-statement/fl-01-workflow-audit.md) — zero of my 12 tasks landed here, intentionally; none of my recurring work is low-enough stakes to hand off unchecked.

**Source(s):** Ethan Mollick, ["On-boarding your AI Intern"](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/on-boarding-your-ai-intern)

## Feynman Rule

**Plain explanation:** If you can't explain a concept in simple terms a complete beginner would understand, you haven't actually understood it yourself — teaching it simply is the real test of understanding, not being able to use jargon fluently.

**Where I've used/seen it:** Core working principle set in my Claude Project instructions from day one — applied throughout FL-01 and every session since.

**Source(s):** Commonly attributed to physicist Richard Feynman's teaching philosophy.

## Build in Public

**Plain explanation:** Sharing progress, decisions, and even mistakes openly while working — not just posting a polished result at the end. The value is in the process being visible, not just the outcome.

**Where I've used/seen it:** One of my own stated internship goals (LinkedIn posts on learnings); also referenced as a general practice within FlyRank's community culture.

**Source(s):** General industry/community practice, not a single canonical source.
