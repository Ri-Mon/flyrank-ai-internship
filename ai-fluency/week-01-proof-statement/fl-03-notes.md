# FL-03 — What Are You Proving

- **Assignment:** FL-03 — Proof Statement
- **Status:** ✅ Complete

## Goal

Finalize a proof statement and one-line why: one claim, one specific person, one action — the foundation the rest of the portfolio build proves against.

## What I built

Full deliverable: [`fl-03-proof-statement.md`](./fl-03-proof-statement.md)

- Finalized proof statement, arrived at through a multi-round interview process: initial vague claims → narrowed audience → evidence check against actual projects → CTA trade-off analysis → length cut → peer-review integration
- Finalized one-line why

## Prompts used, and why

- Iterative interview-style prompting ("what's the real evidence behind this claim?") rather than asking AI to generate a proof statement outright — matches the brief's explicit instruction to use AI as interviewer, not author.

## What I changed or rejected from AI's output

- Rejected the first two proof statement attempts for stacking multiple claims behind "and" — one version listed five virtues in one sentence, another named four audience types in one sentence. Both violated the brief's "one claim" bar.
- Rejected "the few worth watching" wording (a peer-review catch) — implied a quality judgment the actual sort/filter project doesn't make; replaced with "easier to explore."
- Kept "hiring manager" as the one person, even under peer pressure to widen it to include recruiters/founders — widening the audience would have undercut the whole point of narrowing it in the first place.

## QA / verification notes

- Checked against the assignment's own pass/revise criteria: one claim, one person, one action, and the statement could only describe this specific proof — passes.

## Reflection

The real work this week wasn't writing a sentence — it was catching the same trap (vague claims, stacked audiences, unproven scope) resurface in slightly different forms across multiple rounds, including in a peer-suggested CTA change and later in FL-02's pressure test. Narrowing isn't a one-time move; it has to be defended repeatedly as new drafts and outside opinions try to widen it back out.

## Decisions made this session

- **"Book a call" as the CTA**, over "email me" or "invite me to interview" — durable enough (it drives the whole site's design through Week 10) to warrant its own record; see [ADR-0002](../../docs/decisions/0002-book-a-call-cta.md).
- Kept "hiring manager" as the sole audience, resisting peer pressure to widen it — scoped judgment call, not separately recorded as an ADR (same bar FL-01 set: this affects wording/framing, not repo or site architecture).
