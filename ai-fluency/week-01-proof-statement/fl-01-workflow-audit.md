## 1. Workflow Audit

The goal of this audit is to identify recurring activities where AI can meaningfully improve my workflow while ensuring I continue developing the underlying skills myself.

I mapped 21 recurring tasks from my actual week (study, internship work, side projects, and professional habits like LinkedIn), then narrowed to the 12 below — cutting tasks that were redundant or too similar in scope. Each is classified using the task-delegation framework from Ethan Mollick's ["On-boarding your AI Intern"](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/on-boarding-your-ai-intern), adapted to FlyRank's four categories: **Just me**, **Delegate to AI with review**, **Collaborate with AI**, **Fully automate**.

| # | Task | Classification | Rationale |
|---|------|----------------|-----------|
| 1 | Planning my weekly schedule and reviewing progress | Just me | I know my energy patterns, deadlines, and priorities better than any AI can track. |
| 2 | Completing AI Fluency and technical courses | Just me | Learning requires active engagement — I must absorb concepts myself to build foundational knowledge. |
| 3 | Clarifying the task requirements I don't understand | Collaborate with AI | I ask and push back until it clicks — AI stays in the loop, not just checked at the end. |
| 4 | Solving a specific issue in a task or project | Collaborate with AI | I bring project context and logic; AI brings alternative approaches — we iterate together for the best solution. |
| 5 | Researching a new tech concept (e.g., MCP, RAG) | Delegate to AI with review | AI can summarize and find resources quickly, but I must verify accuracy with official documentation. |
| 6 | Brainstorming new project ideas | Collaborate with AI | I have interests and constraints; AI has pattern recognition — together we generate better concepts. |
| 7 | Writing code for front-end projects | Collaborate with AI | I design the logic; AI helps with syntax and boilerplate as we build. |
| 8 | Reviewing my own code for bugs | Collaborate with AI | I understand the intent; AI spots patterns I might miss — pair debugging makes us both better. |
| 9 | Organizing and documenting project work | Delegate to AI with review | AI suggests structure and templates, but I verify it reflects my actual work. |
| 10 | Creating LinkedIn posts about my learnings & experiences | Collaborate with AI | I provide authentic stories and emotions; AI helps with structure and clarity for maximum reach. |
| 11 | Connecting with peers | Just me | Building genuine professional relationships requires my authentic voice and intentional outreach. |
| 12 | Responding to LinkedIn comments/DMs | Just me | Authentic engagement and relationship-building can't be outsourced to AI. |

**Distribution:** Just me (4) · Delegate with review (2) · Collaborate (6) · Fully automate (0)

Most of my recurring work falls into "Collaborate with AI," reflecting my goal of using AI as a working partner rather than a replacement for my own decision-making.

**Note on "Fully automate":** No task was classified this way, intentionally. Per Mollick's own framework, fully automated (unreviewed) AI use is currently a small and risky category — none of my recurring tasks are low-enough stakes to hand off without any check.

## 3. Three Target Tasks (Reused in FL-02 to FL-04)

These three were chosen because they're genuinely recurring across the next three weeks (not one-off decisions), and each has a clear, checkable definition of "done well" — so I can actually tell whether my AI collaboration is improving, not just assume it is.

### Target A — Researching a new tech concept

**Done Well When:**
- I can explain the concept in my own words in 2-3 sentences, without notes
- I've documented at least one meaningful takeaway (more if the topic naturally supports it), written with enough depth that I could explain it to someone else without re-researching
- Notes start in my personal knowledge base and get moved into my public repository when I judge them useful to others

### Target B — Writing code for front-end projects

**Done Well When:**
- I can explain *why* AI suggested a given approach — not just accept it
- The code I ship reflects my own logic/structure decisions, with AI handling syntax/boilerplate, not the other way around
- I catch at least one AI-suggested pattern that doesn't fit my project before it goes in

### Target C — Reviewing my own code for bugs

**Done Well When:**
- For each bug found, I can articulate the root cause, not just the symptom, before applying the fix
- I verify the fix manually (browser/console check), since I don't have a test suite yet — a known gap I'm flagging honestly rather than hiding

## 4. Why These Three

These tasks represent the core loop of my internship:

1. **Learn** → Researching New Concepts (Target A)
2. **Build** → Writing Front-End Code (Target B)
3. **Refine** → Reviewing and Debugging My Own Code (Target C)

Each has a measurable outcome I can track and compare across FL-02, FL-03, and FL-04 to see whether my AI collaboration technique is actually improving — not just producing more output.
